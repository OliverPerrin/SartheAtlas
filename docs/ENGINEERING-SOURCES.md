# Engineering sources and representation boundary

Audit date: 2026-10-01. Scope: the 18 car families in Sarthe Atlas, with their displayed racing variants. The content lives in `src/data/engineeringDetails.ts`.

## What this layer establishes

The cited manufacturer, component supplier and organiser material establishes named architectures and selected components. The cutaway remains a procedural assembly study: no factory CAD, homologation drawings, measurement scans or licensed detailed interior assets were obtained. More mesh detail does not imply measured accuracy.

Each component carries `documented` or `representative` evidence. A documented label means its existence or architectural role is supported, **not** that its mesh shape, location, orientation or connections match a production drawing. Representative parts explain an engineering function; their exact installation on the selected car is unverified. System-level source links provide vehicle context and do not certify every illustrative subpart. The UI should preserve both labels and the geometry notice.

## Verified exceptions used by geometry

- Alpine A424: **single turbo**, not a universal pair. Alpine's live development release identifies the single-turbo V6; ORECA's indexed 2025 sheet supplies the 95-degree bank angle and dry-sump/pushrod details.
- Toyota TR010: double wishbones and pushrods with torsion bars. This is the 2026 technical specification, not a guessed carry-over from its predecessor.
- Valkyrie race car: pushrod torsion-bar suspension with side and central dampers. Do not import the road car's hybrid drive or active ride-height system.
- Porsche 992 GT3 R: front double wishbones, rear multi-link, front fuel cell, rear flat-six. Its gearbox sits ahead of the rear engine.
- LMDh: Bosch MGU and power electronics integrate with the rear drivetrain. Xtrac documents the P1359's transverse three-shaft layout and structural case. No front electric axle is generated for this ruleset.
- Ferrari 296 GT3 and all other non-hybrid entries: no traction battery, inverter, MGU, HV loom or hybrid cooling circuit. A combustion-car low-voltage battery/electrical loom would be a different system.
- Naturally aspirated engines: no turbochargers or charge-air coolers. BMW M4 P58: one inline cylinder bank. Porsche: opposed banks.
- Lexus: the public baseline confirms a six-speed sequential gearbox, but the cited release does not establish a rear transaxle. Its placement remains explicitly unverified.

## Advanced interpretation

The component roles explain load and energy paths: pushrod/rocker motion ratios; independent spring and damping functions; anti-roll versus common-mode heave motion; friction/regen brake blending; differential and half-shaft torque paths; turbo hot-side versus charge-air routing; dry-sump scavenge versus pressure feed; and cockpit control, data and service interfaces. A generic heave element is labelled as a study, not installed equipment, unless the source explicitly establishes it.

Open housings exposes additional representative engine and gearbox mechanisms. Cylinder counts and bank orientations follow the catalogue; crank phase, bore/stroke, piston crowns, two-cam/four-valve arrangements and internal clearances are illustrative. The six/seven forward gear count follows the stated specification, while tooth counts, ratios, dog geometry and shift positions remain studies. The internal topology is static, not a firing-order, combustion or synchronised shift simulation. Closed housings do not render these extra internal meshes.

Study gears use sampled involute flanks and a 20-degree reference pressure angle, with simplified root fillets and machined-looking edge bevels. The generic geometry follows [KHK's gear dimension reference](https://khkgears.net/new/gear_knowledge/gear_technical_reference/calculation_gear_dimensions.html); this does not establish any racing gearbox's actual tooth profile, module, pressure angle or ratios.

Brake migration is described as the changing front/rear braking contribution during a stop. No proprietary migration map, ABS setting, brake pressure, spring rate, damper shim stack, fuel capacity, tyre pressure, electrical voltage or event-specific BoP/deployment threshold is inferred from a mesh. The visible display is a static study graphic. Harnesses, cage joints and extinguisher meshes are not safety-engineering instructions.

## Retrieval and reuse limitations

Manufacturer pages were searched and the relevant passages inspected through live web search/open. The ORECA 2022 and 2025 PDF specifications were available as indexed primary-source text, while direct PDF requests returned 404. These limitations are retained in the register; the older catalogue's ORECA 2017 PDF also returned 404 and was not used as fresh verification. ORECA's 2025 sheet describes the A424 gearbox as longitudinal, while the actual gearbox supplier describes the P1359 as transverse; the supplier's internal gearbox architecture is used and the car's longitudinal powertrain orientation should not be confused with its gear-shaft arrangement.

The Porsche source is the 2023 992 baseline; Lexus is the 2017 GT3 baseline. Those do not prove every subsequent LMGT3 update. The Ford page establishes the baseline architecture rather than every 2026 Evo part. Manufacturer photo galleries and Bosch's cutaway illustrations are reference material only; none were downloaded or redistributed as application assets. Public visibility does not grant general image/model reuse rights.

The Monid skill was inspected and its catalogue discovery was run before generic retrieval. The available dedicated web tool performed the source lookups; no paid Monid execution or CAD purchase was made.
